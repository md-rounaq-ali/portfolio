Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (C-DAC) July 7–8, 2026 Team Kernel Knights 11 Findings Submitted & Evaluated

The Challenge

The C-DAC BOSS OS Bug Bounty Challenge 2026 was a nationwide 36-hour authorized security assessment organized by the Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (C-DAC), a premier R&D organization under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), Government of India.

The challenge brought together ethical hackers, security analysts, and computer science researchers across India to audit and harden BOSS OS (Bharat Operating System Solutions), India's sovereign Linux distribution developed for government and defense deployments.

Assessment Scope & Methodology

Operating inside an authorized assessment environment, our team systematically probed multiple operating system layers:

Focus Areas: Authentication mechanisms, package manager integrity checks, kernel module privileges, desktop environment service isolation, and local privilege escalation vectors.

Team Submissions & Evaluation

Across the 36-hour sprint, team Kernel Knights identified and submitted 11 detailed vulnerability findings complete with steps to reproduce and proof-of-concept exploits:

• 11 of 11 Findings Formally Evaluated: Every submission was triaged and thoroughly reviewed by the official C-DAC jury.
• Vulnerability Verification: Earned an accepted vulnerability verdict with a severity score of 6.30, providing actionable remediation guidance for the BOSS OS core development team.
• Hands-on Threat Modeling: Deepened our team's expertise in Linux kernel security, process privilege auditing, and authorized vulnerability disclosure workflows.
